Reading

In this period of time, I read three books: The Stranger, The Odyssey, and a book by a Mexican writer, Cuentos para Monstruos by Santiago Gonzáles Pedraza. A friend lent me the last one.

I liked The Odyssey very much. When I finished it, I got into mythology. I love it, but sometimes it confuses me because of the huge amount of stories it has.

The Stranger took me by surprise with that ending, and just like my surprise, that feeling is also captured in the book... those moments when, in such a short time, a lot can happen. I saw people have two views of the story: one where the protagonist, Meursault, is seen as a nihilistic, apathetic bad person, and another where he is seen as a man misunderstood by society. Personally, I see him with empathy, but I also believe a lot in adaptation. Sometimes we dont want to do things, but we have to, maybe for survival or moral reasons. I think the book exaggerates those feelings in Meursault to communicate its message, but I also think human emotions cant be ignored like Meursault does.

Cuentos para Monstruos covers themes of trauma, anger, frustration, and human emotions in general. As the name suggests, they are short stories; the book is a compilation of them, and it has a good amount. Some made me think, especially one called País de Pétalos y Velas. It's interesting to see the story from those two perspectives. And another one called Carrito de los Tesoros, which explores the innocence of a child.
